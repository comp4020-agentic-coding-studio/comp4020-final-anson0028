# Play-test by four AI testers, 9 November 2026

Four agents, each driving headless Chromium through Playwright, played a
fresh local server (commit `ea36375`, `WALK_TEST=1` so they could place a
ship when real sailing would have taken too long; every use of that is
marked in their reports) at the same time for about twenty minutes, with
two to five browsers each. None of them had seen the code or the earlier
versions. They were asked to test what the README, the claims and the
design note promise, and to report pass, fail or unclear per item with
what they saw. Their scripts and screenshots are kept outside the repo.
These are agents, not people; what they could not judge is whether any of
it is fun.

## What passed

- Arrival, card, naming, guide line and box, boarding and stepping off,
  the guide ending at the first held station, labels and the crew line,
  the order of jobs, the mobile layout and controls (A: 10 of 12).
- Sails (main 0 to 100% in 4.5 s; main alone 0.47 tiles/s, both sails
  0.71), helm, calm water at the berth and drift outside it, leaving the
  berth, the berth countdown and the new ship, landing with flag, board
  line and visit count surviving a reload, aground and chart-edge
  notices, the chart only at the table, ships stopping short (B: 8 of 8).
- A word heard by the whole deck and not the jetty and the reverse,
  gestures by place and the 400 ms limit, jobs for one to five and the
  move-up, nudges after about ten seconds and none for a dry pump, reef
  damage, holes, patching with its percentage, the pump, sinking with
  the wreck line on the board, notes in every clause (C: 22 of 26).
- Keyboard reach and names on every control, a second tab refused with
  the offer of a second bird, a dropped connection put back aboard, the
  pages and 404s, no cookie or map in any of 751 frames, a move visible
  in 152 ms and a word in 130 ms, no console errors (D: 10 of 12).

## What failed, and what changed the same night

| Found by | Fault | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| D | Enter on a focused button was swallowed by the Enter-opens-chat key | Enter on a button or link is left alone |
| A | A name with no letter was refused by the server but the card closed and kept the random name | The card stays open and says why |
| A | At sea, E off a station said "Nothing to hold here"; the server's "only at the pier" refusal never reached a player | E off a station asks to step off, and the refusal shows |
| A, D | "The ship has left" shown while the ship sat at the berth with a sail rising; E did nothing and said nothing | The guide says the ship is casting off, or still clearing the berth, or when the next comes |
| C | The say box said nothing about who hears until you had boarded and stepped off; the long placeholder was cut | "Talk to your ship" / "Talk to the shore" |
| C | The pump-and-patching chip went red while its owner was patching, because no client knew who was patching | The snapshot carries who is patching |
| C | A "Your job is…" nudge queued before a sinking appeared on the jetty after it | Dropped at the sinking |
| C | The berth sign hung above the mast, unreadable | It hangs by the gangway |
| A, D | At 1100 by 700 the board covered the status; on a phone the Say button overlapped the d-pad and the guide covered the open board and the toast | Width limits, the guide lower on phones, the open board on top |
| B | An empty ship with no sail up still wandered with the drift, so a hull left in the lane swung in and out of the way | No sail, no wander |
| B | "Helm — drifting" at the berth, where the water is flat | Only outside the calm water |
| B | A ship passing an island stern-first was recorded as landing, against the rule that says the bow | The bow, and only the bow |
| A, B | An empty ship left near the berth blocked it for five minutes | A minute (changed earlier the same night) |

## What is still open

- Everyone arrives on the same few tiles and the name labels pile up
  until people move.
- Land is drawn only within sight, so a ship can be "aground" with no
  land on screen; the chart table shows it.
- The wreck marker says "4 aboard", not the names; the board has them.
- Two ships placed overlapping by the test hook pass through each other;
  it cannot happen by sailing.
- A landing fires when the bow is within range of the shore, and
  "Aground" follows seconds later if the helm is not turned: two messages
  for one arrival.
- With eight testers on one berth, a ship was taken within twelve
  seconds of appearing; the queue is the jetty.
