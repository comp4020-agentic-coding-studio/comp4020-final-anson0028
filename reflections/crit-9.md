# Crit 9 — Starting again, on purpose

**The breakthrough.**

The gate worked and the pod was bored. One pair opened it, and everyone
else had nothing to do but walk. I had borrowed Big Walk's distance rule
and, without noticing, its biggest complaint: no solo mode, and puzzles
sized for a fixed group. The research made that plain. Reviewers said it
about Big Walk; the crit pod said it about me.

The breakthrough was a sentence in the decision record: one person can do
everything, but only from where they stand. It keeps the rule the project
began with, that what you can do depends on where your body is, and it
makes headcount matter without the server ever counting heads. A second tab
is just a second body that still has to stand somewhere. Once that was
written, the ship, the stations and the sea that gets harder further out
followed from it.

**What it changed.**

I had the design written before any code this time, and corrected it
before anything was built. Three things changed in that round that I would
otherwise have built wrong: the view, the pier, and solo play. Writing the
document first felt slower and wasn't.

Two habits came out of this week. When I borrow an idea, I read its critics
before its fans, because the fault comes with it. And I stopped asserting
what the game does with two or four people. The ladder is a claim in the
README until the simulated crews run, and it is marked as one.
